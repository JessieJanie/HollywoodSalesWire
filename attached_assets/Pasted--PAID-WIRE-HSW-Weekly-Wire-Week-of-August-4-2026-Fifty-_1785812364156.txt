2. HSW EXCLUSIVE — New Mexico: 828 Productions Quietly Files Two Project Vehicles, Five Months After Its Messy Split With Las Cruces

WHO: 828 Productions, LLC — the boutique film finance/production company founded by Todd Lundbohm in 2019, with credits across 20 films including The Last Son, Assassin Club, and The Price We Pay. Based at 106 S Miranda St, Las Cruces, NM 88005.

WHAT HAPPENED: On July 29, two new LLCs — CL Media P1, LLC and REV Media P1, LLC — were filed with the NM Secretary of State on the same day, both listing 828 Productions, LLC as registered agent (consecutive entity numbers 0008116529/30). The context makes this a story: on February 2, the Las Cruces City Council voted to end its three-year public partnership with 828 and sought the return of roughly $1.3 million in public funds. These paired "P1" filings are the first new project-vehicle activity visible on the registry since that split — the naming pattern (two projects, "phase 1") reads as a slate moving into setup, now on private money.

WHY MONEY MOVES: A production company rebuilding after losing municipal backing needs the full vendor stack fresh: financing partners, bonding, insurance, payroll, crew, post. And vendors who were paid through the city arrangement should re-paper against the new entities.

WHO SHOULD CALL: New Mexico crew services, camera/grip/electric rental in the Las Cruces–El Paso corridor, production insurance and payroll firms, and completion-bond providers. Direct contact: 828 Productions — 575-323-1895, info@828productions.com, 106 S Miranda St, Las Cruces, NM 88005.

Source: HSW Weekly Wire, from NM Secretary of State registry records (July 29, 2026); Las Cruces Sun-News, February 4, 2026 (city partnership termination); 828productions.com.

4. GOD OF WAR (Amazon MGM / Sony Pictures TV) — Lead Recast Forces Mid-October Restart in Vancouver

WHO: God of War — Amazon MGM Studios and Sony Pictures Television with PlayStation Productions; showrunner Ronald D. Moore; two-season back-to-back order. Shooting in and around North Vancouver (production began February at Greenwood Park and the North Vancouver woods) under the working title "Anaheim Realm."

WHAT HAPPENED: Ryan Hurst tore a bicep on set in late June, four months into the shoot. Dave Bautista is in negotiations to take over as Kratos; prep is expected to resume mid-August for a mid-October restart.

WHY MONEY MOVES: A lead recast on a two-season order means re-mobilizing the machine: re-costuming a physically different lead, partial reshoots, revised stunt/training programs, extended stage and location holds, and renewed local contracting. Mid-August prep is the window that opens now.

WHO SHOULD CALL — by category:

Stunt/fight choreography, costume and prosthetics fabrication, camera/grip/lighting rental (North Vancouver): the production office operates under the working title "Anaheim Realm" — that's the name it books under and the name on the DGC BC production list.
Stage and location services: Sony Pictures Television physical production is the managing studio side; Amazon MGM Studios holds the platform relationship.
Insurance: an injury-driven shutdown and restart means cast-insurance and completion terms are being re-papered — brokers with BC production experience should be in these conversations this month.
Source: Deadline, August 3, 2026 (single-sourced on Bautista as of press time; Variety matched same day); production/location details via BC filming notices, February 2026.

6. PARAMOUNT + WBD — Earnings This Week Under a TRO; Deal Pause Extended to Mid-August

WHO: Paramount (reports Tuesday) and Warner Bros. Discovery (reports Thursday, August 6 — confirmed by the company), under a temporary restraining order while 12 state AGs and the WGA pursue antitrust claims. The deal pause was extended in late July to mid-August; no trial date set as of press time.

WHAT HAPPENED: First earnings calls since the TRO. Consensus: Paramount roughly flat at ~$6.9B revenue with EPS compressing; WBD down to ~$9.2B with a small per-share loss. Paramount stock is down ~40% since the February announcement.

WHY MONEY MOVES: Two scenarios, one instruction. If litigation drags into 2027, both studios keep operating — and buying — separately under mounting cost pressure. If the deal closes, vendor consolidation historically takes 12–18 months and procurement can freeze or re-source fast. Either way, vendors who maintain contacts at both studios through the uncertainty are positioned for either outcome. Listen to both calls for greenlight-pace and capital-allocation language.

WHO SHOULD CALL: Every vendor on either studio's active slate — listening duty this week. Production financing and banking contacts: watch for debt-paydown guidance tied to asset sales.

HSW will track this merger from the only angle that matters to our readers — the sales opportunities. Every issue will flag what each development means for vendor contracts, procurement freezes, and re-papering windows as they open.

Source: Deadline, August 3, 2026; WBD investor relations, August 2026; Bloomberg, July 23, 2026 (pause extension).
