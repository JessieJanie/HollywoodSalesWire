Karilyn's copy direction (July 30, 2026) for the next pass on Hollywood Sales Wire sales material.

## The core line (verbatim — the keeper)
"Leave it to the hot-lead pros to deliver the hottest sales leads."

Customer language, not investor language. Use it (and variations) as the moat argument. Do NOT replace it with feature lists or technical explanations of how the research machine works.

## Psychology to preserve
The framing makes NOT subscribing the risky-looking choice. Example register:
"We watch seven studio job boards and five state incentive programs continuously, so your salespeople call the moment money moves."
Reader thinks "my competitor might already get this," not "I could build that." Fear of missing the lead beats pride of DIY.

One framing serves three jobs:
- Launch argument — subscribe now because money is moving now.
- Price justification — paying for the machine that never stops watching, not a newsletter.
- Renewal argument — cancel and the watching stops while your competitor's doesn't.

## Honest version of the moat (background; keeps copy true)
Nothing is secret or impossible to replicate. What can't be cheaply replicated:
- Accumulated gotcha knowledge (fake job-board counts on empty searches, reappearing stories on re-crawls, bot-blocking state sites) already encoded in the pipeline; a DIYer discovers these silently, weeks in.
- Maintenance treadmill — sources change; our system notices when a source quietly breaks; DIY scripts rot in silence.
- Economics — engineer-months to rebuild is a bad trade. Bloomberg moat: nothing is magic, nobody sane rebuilds it.

## Second USP — "we built the tool" (added later July 30, 2026)
Karilyn built the search tool HSW uses, purpose-built for exactly this ultra-precise, deepest-possible-dive research HSW delivers monthly. Framing: HSW isn't using just any reader for research — it's one we know inside out and adjust as needed to keep delivering the hottest leads to be found. Reinforces the moat: the machine is ours, tuned continuously. (Still bound by the guardrails below — "not worth replicating," never "nobody else has this.")

## Guardrails (house rules still govern)
- "Seven studio job boards and five state incentive programs" is factual today — if coverage changes, the number changes. Every quantified claim stays literally true.
- No "nobody else has this" claims — moat is "not worth replicating," never "impossible to replicate."
- Enemy frame stays the rented list / stale directory, per launch copy.
- All existing pricing and offer locks remain exactly as agreed.

**How to apply:** On the next copy pass, draft with this framing (a consideration, not a mandate — Karilyn expects more angles to accumulate and be used IN ADDITION before the pass runs), then run the 9.5-or-better rewrite procedure: rate the draft 1–10 with a summary of strengths and weaknesses, rewrite using that summary, repeat until it rates 9.5+. Copy remains publisher-locked — no edits without explicit request.

## Industry-contraction framing (Aug 15, 2026, from Karilyn)
She shared an essay arguing Hollywood's human production class is being hollowed out (AI + production flight from LA) while the film business itself grows. Her stance: "HSW just keeps following the money for those still in and newcomers coming into the industry."
**Why:** durable editorial/copy angle — contraction makes buying signals MORE valuable (fewer, faster windows; can't afford cold leads). HSW sells navigation of the emerging industry, not nostalgia for the old one.
**How to apply:** future sales copy and Wire/social framing may lean on this; never doom-tone — Karilyn's brand is opportunity-for-those-still-selling.
