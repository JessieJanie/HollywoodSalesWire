# Hollywood book brainstorm (as of Aug 2, 2026 — discussion only, no work started)

## Karilyn's goal
A nonfiction book (Amazon print-on-demand) that (a) can sell on its own and (b) supports HSW sales. Her seed insight: **consumers pay twice** — taxes fund productions via incentives, then they pay at the box office. She prefers pushback; wants honest creative partnership.

## Where the discussion landed
- **Subject (narrowed, my strong recommendation she seemed to accept):** NOT "a fresh look at Hollywood / how the money works" — that category is owned (Epstein's *The Hollywood Economist*, Ben Fritz's *The Big Picture*). The unclaimed subject is **taxpayer money: the 50-state bidding war for productions, billions in public film incentives, all sitting in public records nobody reads.** Framing: "Hollywood's silent business partner: you."
- **Tone: seduction, not outrage.** Her instinct, affirmed. Model = *Moneyball*: no villains, just a smarter way to read the numbers; whoever reads them first wins. "Pay twice" is the chapter-one hook, not the grievance driving the book. Outrage would also clash with HSW's brand (members sell TO Hollywood).
- **The trough question:** incentives are NOT majors-only. Most state programs open to any qualifying production; CA has a dedicated indie set-aside; but majors feed better because they staff professional incentive departments. Honest framing: "the trough is open, but only the majors brought professional eaters." Serves both general readers and HSW's audience.
- **Tension spine (needed for page-turning):** something isn't what it appears — states claiming returns economists can't find; governors ratcheting the bidding war; indie carve-outs underused.
- **Faces needed:** book needs people, not just receipts — state negotiators, an indie producer who found the carve-out, a soundstage town. Interview work = the one muscle HSW's research machine can't supply.

## HSW's research advantage (why this book is nearly a byproduct of the business)
1. Monday pipeline already reads award lists weekly (CA approved projects, NY ESD, state film offices) and diffs across weeks — accumulating a money-movement record nobody else has.
2. Can run pattern questions across hundreds of public documents (e.g., share of CA credits going to single-project companies) — where fresh insights live.
3. Receipts / "single source — verify" discipline already institutional.
Caveats given: moat is connecting-not-collecting (same as HSW's moat); raw-page archive keeps ~5 weeks (deliberate), but states publish multi-year archives so historical depth is pointable, not blocked.

## Structure sketch floated
Part 1 — the seduction (why every state wants Hollywood; bidding wars; pay-twice math as fascinating fact). Part 2 — the players (how majors work the system; the unused indie carve-outs). Part 3 — follow the money yourself (public records, the method — Field Guide DNA, ending at HSW's doorstep).

## Data-spine test (DONE Aug 2, 2026 — verdict: GO)
Ran 3 pattern questions on CA's full archive (907 projects, FY2009-10→2025-26). Findings memo: `docs/film-incentive-pattern-test-memo.md`. Surprises: 86% of company names appear once but take only 26% of dollars (concealment is naming, not fragmentation — HBO's shows collect $183M as "Cooler Waters Productions, LLC"); indie share sat at/below the legal set-aside 2015-2021 (2.7% low in 2020-21) then stampeded post-expansion; avg award $2.1M→$9.0M, credit-per-hire tripled, 34/175 projects "Untitled" in 2025-26 vs 0 in 2015-16; largest-ever award $48M to "Unt. DET Project 13". Next chapter test: same questions on NY ESD quarterly PDFs.

## Original agreed next step (context)
Run 2–3 "pattern questions" against state incentive archives to test whether the data yields genuine surprises. Surprises = chapter one + press hook. No surprises = a day spent, not a year. Bestseller strategy: own a niche Amazon category durably rather than chase a spike; chapters double as Field Guide articles for site SEO; numbers must be literally true and sourced (same rule as the Wire).
